Textbook Analytical Guide: Imperfect vs. Perfect Competition Shut-Down Rule

Why the shut-down point under monopoly / monopolistic competition occurs to the left of minimum AVC

Key Exam Distinction: Tangency of AR & AVC
1. Exact Shut-Down Point P* = AVC*

At Q* (where MR = MC), the downward-sloping D = AR curve is tangent to the AVC curve. Total Revenue exactly covers Total Variable Cost (TR = TVC), leaving a loss equal to Fixed Cost (-TFC). The firm is indifferent between operating and shutting down.

2. Immediate Shut-Down P* < AVC*

The entire Demand (AR) curve lies below AVC. Even at the loss-minimizing positive output where MR = MC, price cannot cover average variable costs (TR < TVC). Operating adds uncovered variable losses on top of TFC, so the firm shuts down immediately (Q = 0).

3. Operate at a Loss AVC* < P* < ATC*

Although the firm incurs an economic loss (P* < ATC*), price exceeds AVC*. Each unit sold generates a positive contribution margin (P* - AVC* > 0) that pays for part of Fixed Costs, making the operating loss smaller than -TFC.

Why Tangency Occurs Left of Min AVC: Since MR = P + Q·(dP/dQ) and MC = AVC + Q·(dAVC/dQ), when MR = MC and P = AVC at Q* > 0, their slopes must be equal: dP/dQ = dAVC/dQ < 0. Because Demand slopes downward (-b < 0), tangency must occur on the downward-sloping portion of AVC (to the left of minimum AVC).
